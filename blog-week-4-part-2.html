WEEK 4, PART 2: THE FINAL SCOREBOARD

By Buzz·PUBLISHED Monday, November 23, 2026·UPDATED November 23, 2026·5 MIN READ

Paper Mode v1 is graded, all fifteen days of it. The market shadow won the run, our two biggest tickets were lottery-class longshots, and Scout closed on the only green streak of the experiment. Here's the whole scoreboard — losers included, like always.

Key insights
  • Final standings: Market shadow −$21.95 · Bright −$33.52 · Scout −$414.50. The shadow hit 60.8% of its brackets and still lost a little — that's how hard this game is priced.
  • The two biggest winning tickets of the run were the longshots: Louisville +$72 and Dallas +$27.
  • Scout finished with four straight green days (+$33.10), including the best day of the run — after the rules changed, not before.
  • The exit test's honest answer: we rode everything to settlement. Exit strategies are now being tested properly, in simulation, in Paper Mode v2.

The shadow — a portfolio that does nothing but buy the market's favorite bracket in every city — won the run. It was right six times out of ten at an average price of about 59¢, priced almost exactly at what it delivered, and it still finished slightly underwater. Fees make it plainer: across the ten fully instrumented days, Scout and Bright together lost $447.46 after fees; the shadow lost $44.19.

That's the bar. Beating this market doesn't mean being different from it — Nov 9 and Nov 10, when Scout went 0-for-24 twice being different, are what that costs. It means being right exactly where the market is wrong.

THE HIGHLIGHTS

The run's biggest single tickets were the ones this desk flags as lottery-class — far from the model's center, bought because the price was wrong, not because they were likely:

  • Louisville, Nov 5 — Bright, +$72. The biggest ticket of the experiment, and a disagreement pick.
  • Dallas, Nov 7 — Scout, +$27. Scout's biggest of the run.

And the finish: Nov 11–14, four straight green days, +$33.10 — capped by Nov 13, when Scout hit 10 of 24 brackets for +$13.32, the best day of the entire run. The system ended its test period improving. It still failed its test. Both things are true, and this journal prints both.

THE EXIT TEST, IN ONE BREATH

Paper Mode had a pre-registered test for whether it earned the right to end: beat the market on forecast skill, on money after fees, and do it without the two biggest tickets carrying the result. It failed all three.

The plain version of what that means for exits: throughout v1, we rode every position to settlement. No cashing out early, no stops. The intraday records said value kept showing up before 2 PM and settlement kept only part of it — but a replay of the alternatives never produced a rule that made the book profitable, so no exit rule was adopted on a hunch.

That question is now being tested the way it should be: Paper Mode v2's arenas run the same entries under six different exit rules, side by side, in simulation. When one of them earns it — ten-plus slates, ahead after fees, not carried by one day — it gets proposed for live use. Until then: ride to settlement.
