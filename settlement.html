THE NUMBER THAT PAYS YOU ISN'T THE ONE ON YOUR SCREEN

Kalshi's daily temperature markets settle on a single whole-degree number that most traders never look at directly. This is where that number comes from, whose name is on it, and why the feed you watched all afternoon can be right while your bracket still loses.

By Buzz — who trades these markets daily under that name in Kalshi's own chat rooms, and whose desk's complete record is published on this site. November 2026.

KEY INSIGHTS

  • Kalshi's daily temperature markets settle on the high The Weather Company publishes for the market's named station — not on your weather app, not on a live feed, and not, since August 2026, directly on the National Weather Service.
  • The Weather Company's number is taken from the NWS daily climate report (the CLI) for that station. The CLI underneath hasn't changed — the name on the door has.
  • The first final report is the one that pays. A correction that lands after expiration does not reopen your market.
  • The settlement day runs on local standard time, all year. During daylight saving, that's 1:00 AM to 1:00 AM on your wall clock — an hour later than most traders assume.
  • The published value is a whole degree, built from the station's continuous record — so the decimal feed you watched all afternoon can be honest and still not be the number.
01

THE SHORT ANSWER

A Kalshi daily-high market pays on one whole-degree number: the highest temperature The Weather Company publishes for that market's named station, for a day that runs midnight to midnight in local standard time. That published number comes from the National Weather Service's daily climate report for the station. Everything else — your app, the live observation feed, the forecast you traded on — is information. That one number is the settlement.

If you read nothing else, read your market's own rules before you trade it: every Kalshi market page names its source and its station. This post is how to understand what you're reading there.

02

THE CHAIN OF CUSTODY: HOW A TEMPERATURE BECOMES A SETTLEMENT

Your payout travels through four hands, and only the last one pays you. A sensor at one named station records the day. The National Weather Service compiles that station's day into its daily climate report. The Weather Company publishes the high from that report on its Kalshi portal (weather.com/kalshi). Kalshi settles your contract on the published value.

Two consequences follow from that chain. First, "the temperature in Chicago" is not a thing this market recognizes — the market recognizes one station (for Chicago, Midway; its climate report is CLIMDW), and a warmer reading across town at O'Hare is a rumor. Second, the number is published the next morning, not at midnight — settlement follows the report, and the report follows its own schedule.

03

THE DAY RUNS ON STANDARD TIME — EVEN IN JULY

The settlement day is midnight to midnight in local standard time, and it does not move for daylight saving. The NWS climate day works this way, and Kalshi's daily markets inherit it.

What that means on the wall clock: while your city is on daylight time, the settlement day runs 1:00 AM to 1:00 AM. A warm reading at 12:30 AM on a summer night — which feels like the new day — still belongs to the day before. Trading closes to match: the market's day ends at midnight standard time, which is 1:00 AM daylight time, whatever the close-condition text on the market page seems to say.

This is not trivia. Overnight lows and early-morning warm fronts live exactly in that shifted hour, and every spring and fall, traders in the chat rooms discover the standard-time day the expensive way.

04

FIRST FINAL COUNTS: CORRECTIONS, HOLDS, AND THE ONE EXCEPTION

Only the first official, non-preliminary value is used for settlement. The preliminary climate report — the late-afternoon edition — never settles anything. The first final report does, and revisions published after the market's expiration are not considered. A corrected report next week does not reopen a settled market.

This rule has already decided real money in a way anyone can check. On August 29, 2026, Miami's climate report was issued twice in one morning: a final report showing 90° at 4:24 AM, then a second final showing 85° at 5:10 AM. Kalshi and The Weather Company settled on 90 — the first final. If you held the 85–86 bracket and watched the second report land, the system worked exactly as its rules say it works. The rules just weren't the ones you assumed.

Two wrinkles worth knowing:

  • The material-error exception. Since the September 3, 2026 events, each market's rules carry a paragraph most traders never read: if Kalshi judges the first published value materially wrong, it can hold expiration until a corrected value is published — up to the expiration date — and if no corrected value comes, every bracket resolves to the last fair price. It is a narrow door, held by the exchange, not by you — but it exists.
  • Settlement can simply be slow. When a final report's high disagrees with the station's 6-hour or 24-hour maximums, or comes in lower than the preliminary report's, settlement can wait while the reports are reconciled. A market that hasn't paid by mid-morning hasn't necessarily gone wrong; the referee is checking the tape.
05

WHY THE FEED YOU WATCHED CAN BE RIGHT — AND YOU STILL LOSE

The published high is a whole degree, computed from the station's continuous record — not the highest decimal print you saw scroll by. The live feed shows you observations in tenths, at the moments the station reports. The climate report's high is built from the station's full record, including its one-minute data, and published as an integer. A day whose instruments peaked at 71.6° can publish as 72. A day you watched top out at 69.1 can publish as 69.

Our own graded book carries the second case. On November 7, 2026, in Dallas, our live feed never showed better than 69.1° — and the climate report printed 69, inside the 68–69 bracket, which paid. The feed wasn't lying to us all afternoon. It was answering a slightly different question than the one the market asks. The market asks the report. Grade your day against the report — we publish our record graded exactly that way, and it's the only grading that's honest.

06

READ YOUR OWN MARKET'S RULES (FIVE MINUTES, BEFORE THE TRADE)

Every Kalshi market page carries its own rulebook summary — the source agency, the station, and the expiration terms are printed on the market itself. Before trading a temperature market:

  • Open the market and read its rules section — not a blog post, not this one either. Ours included.
  • Confirm the source agency named first (for daily temperatures today: The Weather Company).
  • Confirm the station — the climate-report code names it (CLINYC is Central Park, CLIMDW is Midway). If the station isn't the one you forecast, your forecast is for a different market.
  • Check the value yourself the next morning at weather.com/kalshi and against the station's CLI — when the two disagree, the first final is the one that paid.

07

QUESTIONS TRADERS ASK ABOUT SETTLEMENT

Does Kalshi settle on the National Weather Service or The Weather Company?
The Weather Company — for daily temperature events from August 14, 2026 onward, market rules name The Weather Company first and the NWS second. The Weather Company's published high is taken from the NWS daily climate report, so the NWS still produces the underlying number; it just isn't the name on the settlement anymore. Older guides that say "settles on NWS" are describing the market as it was.

Why did my bracket lose when my app showed the temperature hit?
Because your app doesn't settle the market. Settlement is one whole-degree value, for one named station, over a standard-time day, as first published in final form. An app reading from a different station, a forecast model, or a rounded consumer feed can show your number all day without the official record ever printing it.

What if the climate report gets corrected after I was paid — or after I lost?
Corrections after expiration don't count. The first final value governs, with one narrow exception: if Kalshi judges that first value materially wrong, its rules let it hold expiration for a corrected value, up to the expiration date. What the exchange will not do is reopen a market for a routine revision.

What time does the Kalshi weather day start and end?
Midnight to midnight local standard time at the station. During daylight saving time, that's 1:00 AM to 1:00 AM on the local clock — so the first hour after midnight belongs to the previous settlement day.

When does my market actually settle?
The morning after the trading day, once the first final value is published — the market's expiration is 10:00 AM ET, and most markets resolve around it. When reports disagree with each other, settlement can slide into the afternoon while they're reconciled.
