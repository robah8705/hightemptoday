KEY FACTS ABOUT DALLAS' HIGH

  • One station sets the number: Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport (KDFW). Not the suburbs, not the reading on your phone.
  • Dallas is a frontal city. DFW sits on open plains between the two downtowns, exposed to Gulf air from the south and Arctic air from the north, and November is when those two start trading days.
  • It reports about every 5 minutes, so you can watch the afternoon climb in close to real time.
  • The print has two steps: a preliminary climate report around 6:28 PM, then the final around 12:39 AM. The market settles on the final.

DALLAS IN LATE NOVEMBER: WHAT THE RECORD SAYS

Across 472 November days in the official record from 2011 to 2026, DFW International's highs averaged 68.6°, with a median of 70° — but one November day in ten came in at 54° or colder, and one in ten reached 81° or warmer. That spread, a standard deviation of 10.5°, is the honest shape of a Dallas November.

This November has run warm: through Tue Nov 24, the month's official highs average 70.7°, 2.1° above that 15-year November average. Zoom in on this exact stretch of the calendar — November 20 through 30, over the last seven years — and the official highs ran from 43° to 83°, with a median of 64°.

THE RULEIn Dallas, price the range, not the point. A November forecast is a center of a very wide distribution, and the brackets near the middle are where that distribution is flattest.

WHY DALLAS' OFFICIAL HIGH COMES FROM THE AIRPORT

Dallas's official weather record is kept at Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport, midway between the two cities at about 600 feet, on open North Texas prairie. It's the station the National Weather Service uses for the Dallas climate report, and the one Kalshi's Dallas high-temperature market names in its rules.

DFW's exposure is its character. Nothing stands between the airport and the Gulf of Mexico to the south or the plains to the north, so the station takes the full force of both air masses — humid southerly warmth one day, a blue norther the next. Downtown readings, hemmed in by buildings, routinely differ by a degree or two.

THE RULEDallas's official high is DFW International's high, as printed in the NWS climate report.

DALLAS: WHERE TWO AIR MASSES TRADE DAYS

North Texas is a borderland. Warm, humid air pushes up from the Gulf; cold, dry air slides down the plains from Canada; and the line between them — the front — parks itself over Dallas often enough that the city can live in two seasons in one week.

  • Frontal passages. A cold front through DFW can drop the temperature 15 degrees in an hour and end the day's climb on the spot. In November, the timing of the front is often the entire forecast question.
  • Gulf return flow. Between fronts, southerly winds haul Gulf moisture back over the plains. Those days build steadily, peak in the early afternoon, and feel like a different month from the frontal days around them.
  • Open-prairie sun. The airport's flat, open setting takes unobstructed sun and wind alike — no lake, no hills, no sea breeze to cap or rescue a day. What the air mass says, the station prints.

You can see the shape of it in the tables above: over the last 30 days, DFW International's official highs ran from 47° to 85°, averaging 72.5°. That is Dallas's character in one line — the rest of this page is about trading it honestly.

THE WRONG-STATION TRAP: LOVE FIELD IS NOT THE NUMBER

  • Dallas Love Field (KDAL) is the classic trap — a full ASOS station minutes from downtown, reporting the same hours, often a degree or two apart from DFW. It is not the settlement station, and the difference has decided brackets.
  • Fort Worth Meacham (KFTW) and Alliance (KAFW) report for the metro's west side, 25-plus miles away, under the same sky but not the same number.
  • Downtown sensors run warm at night and lag the airport's afternoon — useful color, never settlement.

Dallas is a two-airport town with a one-airport market. The bigger, quieter station between the cities is the only one that prints in the climate report.

THE RULEKDFW or it didn't happen. Love Field is the loudest wrong number in Texas — only DFW International is settlement.

WHEN DALLAS' NUMBER BECOMES OFFICIAL

Dallas's climate report comes from the National Weather Service office in Fort Worth, and it lands in two steps. A preliminary report posts in the late afternoon — today's came at 6:28 PM — carrying the day's high as it stands. The final report, covering the full climate day, posts around 12:39 AM. Kalshi settles the Dallas market on The Weather Company's reporting of that final number, in whole degrees.

Two habits worth building: never grade your own day off the preliminary alone, because the final is the one that pays; and remember the climate day runs midnight to midnight standard time, so during daylight saving the official day runs 1 AM to 1 AM on the clock — a warm midnight hour can belong to either day, and the report, not your watch, decides which.

Where is Dallas's official temperature measured?

At Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport, station code KDFW. Dallas's official daily highs and records come from this station.

What time of day does Dallas usually hit its high?

On a typical fall day, between about 1:40 PM and 4:41 PM, most often in the 3:00 PM hour. 9.8% of November highs are set before noon.

Why is Dallas's forecast sometimes off by several degrees?

Because November is when the fronts start running on schedule, and a front that arrives two hours early or late moves the high by double digits. The center of the forecast is usually fair; the timing is the gamble. In the official record, November highs at the airport average about 69°, with a standard deviation of 10.5° — that spread is the honest size of a Dallas November surprise.

Why doesn't my weather app match Dallas's official high?

Your app is usually showing a forecast, a model estimate for your location, or a different sensor. Only DFW International's reading becomes the official high.

When does the official Dallas high come out?

A preliminary climate report usually posts around 6:28 PM. The final report, with the official high for the full climate day, posts around 12:39 AM.
