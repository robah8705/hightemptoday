WE APPLIED FOR NOAA'S 1-MINUTE ASOS FEED. NOAA SAID NO.

By Buzz — HighTempToday

Short version: HighTempToday applied to NOAA's MADIS program for real-time 1-minute ASOS observations — the fastest official temperature feed in the country. NOAA approved us for public access only. The 1-minute data is restricted to government and NOAA users, full stop. So we built Pro on the 5-minute public feed instead — and anyone who tells you their private product runs on real-time 1-minute ASOS data is bluffing, an insider, or selling you model output dressed up as observations.

Key insights
  • We applied on November 15, 2026, as Red Pine LLC. NOAA answered November 16: public access only.
  • Public MADIS ASOS data arrives at the 5-minute mark — 12 observations per hour, per station. The 1-minute observations are restricted to Gov/NOAA users. There is no private application, tier, or fee that unlocks them.
  • HighTempToday Pro runs on the public 5-minute HFMETAR feed — the fastest ASOS data any private operator can get from MADIS.
  • Kalshi settles its daily-high markets on the official NWS climate report, not on any live feed. The feed is for tracking the day as it builds; settlement truth is the printed report.

WHO / HOW / WHY

  • Who: Buzz, of HighTempToday. We track the daily high temperature at all 24 Kalshi settlement stations and publish our full graded record — winners and losers.
  • How: We applied through NOAA's own MADIS data application, under our real company name, stating exactly what the data was for. The reply is quoted below, from MADIS/HADS Support.
  • Why publish a rejection? Because data provenance is the product. If we fudge where our numbers come from, nothing else on this site means anything.

WHAT WE WANTED IT FOR

Our Pro command center tracks each station's high as the day builds. Daily-high markets are decided late: a station's high often prints inside a narrow afternoon window, and a two-degree spike can move a bracket from dead to settled in minutes. One-minute observations would show that spike the moment it happens, instead of up to five minutes later. Across 24 stations, that gap is the difference between watching the day and chasing it. That's what we asked NOAA for — a real-time 1-minute ASOS and METAR feed for a public weather dashboard.

WHAT NOAA SAID — THE RECEIPT

Our application went in on November 15. The answer came back November 16 from Jonathan Truesdell, MADIS/HADS Support:

"Based on your email, we can only provide you with public access to MADIS data."

And on the data itself:

"MADIS ASOS data is only available at the 5min mark — i.e. 12 values per hour per station — the 1-min observations are restricted to Gov/NOAA."

The same email notes that FTP access to MADIS was terminated in April 2026; public data is served over anonymous HTTPS. No ambiguity, no appeal path, no "check back later." Public is what private operators get.

WHO THEY ACTUALLY ACCEPT

Government and NOAA users. That's the list. This isn't a pricing problem — there is no paid tier of MADIS that sells the 1-minute observations to a private company, at any price. We know, because asking was the entire point of the application.

WHAT WE RUN INSTEAD

Pro is live on the public 5-minute HFMETAR feed — the same feed NOAA pointed us to, pulled over anonymous HTTPS, already running in production. Five-minute observations at every settlement station, with the official NWS reports as the settlement record on top. It's the fastest legitimate ASOS feed available to anyone outside the government, and we'd rather show you exactly what it is than pretend it's something faster.

THE BLUFF TEST

So if a dashboard, a Discord, or a data seller tells you their private product runs on real-time 1-minute ASOS observations, one of three things is true:

  1. They're bluffing. It's the same 5-minute public feed everyone gets, relabeled.
  2. They're an insider. Restricted government data doesn't leak into a subscription product by accident — and that's not a business model, it's a liability.
  3. It's algorithmic prediction data. A model interpolating between real observations can print a fresh number every minute. That can be useful. It is not an observation — and interpolation smooths out exactly the spikes that decide these markets.

There's one question that settles it: "Show me your MADIS approval." We can show you ours. It's the email above — and it says no.

We'll keep publishing our receipts: the approvals, the denials, and the graded record, winners and losers. That's the deal here. Pro is live in open beta on the 5-minute feed — hightemptoday.com/pro.html.
