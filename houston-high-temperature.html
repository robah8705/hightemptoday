KEY FACTS ABOUT HOUSTON'S HIGH

  • One station sets the number: William P. Hobby Airport (KHOU). Not the suburbs, not the reading on your phone.
  • Houston is a sea-breeze city. Hobby Airport sits south of downtown, close enough to the Gulf that the afternoon sea breeze regularly arrives and ends the climb — the day's high is often a race between the sun and the breeze.
  • It reports about every 5 minutes, so you can watch the afternoon climb in close to real time.
  • The print has two steps: a preliminary climate report around 4:26 PM, then the final around 1:46 AM. The market settles on the final.

WHY HOUSTON'S OFFICIAL HIGH COMES FROM THE AIRPORT

Houston's official weather record is kept at William P. Hobby Airport, on the city's south side, about 45 feet above sea level in the flat Gulf coastal plain. It's the station the National Weather Service uses for Houston's daily climate report, and the one Kalshi's Houston high-temperature market names in its rules.

Hobby's position matters twice over. It is well south of downtown, closer to Galveston Bay and the Gulf, so it feels the sea breeze earlier and stronger than the rest of the metro. And it is not the airport most Houstonians fly from — that distinction has misled more than one trader reading the wrong feed.

THE RULEHouston's official high is Hobby Airport's high, as printed in the NWS climate report.

HOUSTON: THE SEA BREEZE SETS THE CEILING

Houston's coastal plain is flat, humid and bayou-cut, a short drive from a warm Gulf. The daily rhythm that follows from that geography is one of the most reliable on the board: build through the morning, then wait for the water to answer.

  • The sea breeze. As the land heats, air off the Gulf pushes inland, usually reaching Hobby in the early afternoon. Temperatures flatten or fall within minutes of its arrival — many Houston highs are simply the reading at the moment the breeze landed.
  • Gulf humidity. Houston's air rarely dries out. Humid days climb slower and peak lower than the temperature alone suggests, and the city's highs bunch into a narrower band than the dry-air cities on this board.
  • Bayou and bay effects. Galveston Bay and the ship channel put water on the station's east and south, moderating Hobby against the hotter readings that build over the inland suburbs on calm days.

You can see the shape of it in the tables above: over the last 30 days, Houston Hobby's official highs ran from 60° to 89°, averaging 76.3°. That is Houston's character in one line — the rest of this page is about trading it honestly.

THE WRONG-STATION TRAP: THE BIG AIRPORT IS THE WRONG AIRPORT

  • Houston Intercontinental (KIAH) is the famous trap — the metro's main airport, 25 miles north in the piney woods, routinely a degree or three away from Hobby, sometimes on the other side of a bracket. It settles nothing.
  • Ellington Airport (KEFD), southeast near the bay, feels the sea breeze even earlier than Hobby and runs cooler on breezy afternoons.
  • Sugar Land (KSGR) and the west-side stations bake inland while Hobby is already under the breeze — same metro, different afternoon.

Houston gives a trader two full airport feeds and settles on the smaller, southern one. Knowing which airport is the market is half of trading this city.

WHEN HOUSTON'S NUMBER BECOMES OFFICIAL

Houston's climate report comes from the National Weather Service office in Houston, and it lands in two steps. A preliminary report posts in the late afternoon — today's came at 4:26 PM — carrying the day's high as it stands. The final report, covering the full climate day, posts around 1:46 AM. Kalshi settles the Houston market on The Weather Company's reporting of that final number, in whole degrees.

Two habits worth building: never grade your own day off the preliminary alone, because the final is the one that pays; and remember the climate day runs midnight to midnight standard time, so during daylight saving the official day runs 1 AM to 1 AM on the clock — a warm midnight hour can belong to either day, and the report, not your watch, decides which.

Where is Houston's official temperature measured?

At William P. Hobby Airport, station code KHOU. Houston's official daily highs and records come from this station.

What time of day does Houston usually hit its high?

On a typical fall day, between about 12:30 PM and 4:04 PM, most often in the 3:00 PM hour. 8.6% of November highs are set before noon.

Why is Houston's forecast sometimes off by several degrees?

Because the sea breeze is a timing forecast, and timing is hard — a breeze that lands at noon instead of two costs the day several degrees, and a day it never arrives runs away to the upside. In the official record, November highs at the airport average about 75°, with a standard deviation of 9.2° — that spread is the honest size of a Houston November surprise.

Why doesn't my weather app match Houston's official high?

Your app is usually showing a forecast, a model estimate for your location, or a different sensor. Only Houston Hobby's reading becomes the official high.

When does the official Houston high come out?

A preliminary climate report usually posts around 4:26 PM. The final report, with the official high for the full climate day, posts around 1:46 AM.
