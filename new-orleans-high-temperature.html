NEW ORLEANS IN LATE NOVEMBER: WHAT THE RECORD SAYS

Across 142 November days in the official record from 2011 to 2026, Louis Armstrong International's highs averaged 73.0°, with a median of 75° — but one November day in ten came in at 59° or colder, and one in ten reached 84° or warmer. That spread, a standard deviation of 9.0°, is the honest shape of a New Orleans November.

This November has run cool: through Tue Nov 24, the month's official highs average 71.8°, 1.2° below that 15-year November average. Zoom in on this exact stretch of the calendar — November 20 through 30, over the last seven years — and the official highs ran from 54° to 85°, with a median of 69°.

WHY NEW ORLEANS' OFFICIAL HIGH COMES FROM THE AIRPORT

New Orleans's official weather record is kept at Louis Armstrong New Orleans International Airport, in Kenner, about 11 miles west of the French Quarter, a few feet above sea level between the river and the lake. It's the station the National Weather Service uses for the city's daily climate report, and the one Kalshi's New Orleans high-temperature market names in its rules.

The station's position — flat, low, water on two sides, marsh on all the others — defines its climate. There is no elevation, no valley and no dry air anywhere in its story. What the Gulf sends, Kenner reports.

THE RULENew Orleans's official high is Armstrong International's high, as printed in the NWS climate report.

NEW ORLEANS: HUMID AIR, NARROW NUMBERS

New Orleans lives in a bowl of water — river, lake, marsh and Gulf in every direction. Saturated air resists temperature change the way a flywheel resists a push: slowly, and not far. That physics writes this market's personality.

  • The humidity flywheel. Moist air takes more energy to warm and gives it up slowly at night. New Orleans's daily ranges are the smallest on the board, its highs cluster tightly, and its November standard deviation is a fraction of the plains cities'.
  • Lake and Gulf breezes. Pontchartrain to the north and the Gulf to the south both send breezes that cap the afternoon. On many days the 'peak' is a long, flat shelf from late morning onward, and the exact top degree is nearly a coin flip.
  • The season's first real fronts. November is when continental air first reaches the Gulf Coast with conviction. A strong front can knock New Orleans from the 80s to a high in the 50s the next day — the month's only real source of drama, and it arrives from the north, on schedule or not.

You can see the shape of it in the tables above: over the last 30 days, Louis Armstrong International's official highs ran from 59° to 83°, averaging 73.4°. That is New Orleans's character in one line — the rest of this page is about trading it honestly.

THE WRONG-STATION TRAP: THE LAKEFRONT READS A DIFFERENT DAY

  • New Orleans Lakefront (KNEW) sits directly on Pontchartrain's shore inside the city — breezier, cooler on sunny afternoons, and the number most local displays actually show. It settles nothing.
  • Downtown and French Quarter sensors bake in the urban core's heat and read warmer than the airport's open field in Kenner, especially at night.
  • Slidell and Northshore stations across the lake live on the other side of 24 miles of water, in a breezier, slightly cooler regime of their own.

New Orleans offers a trader a famous in-city airport and a real one out in Kenner. The market chose Kenner, and its humid, narrow numbers are the game.

THE RULEKMSY or it didn't happen. Lakefront is information — only Armstrong International is settlement.

WHEN NEW ORLEANS' NUMBER BECOMES OFFICIAL

New Orleans's climate report comes from the National Weather Service office in New Orleans, and it lands in two steps. A preliminary report posts in the late afternoon — today's came at 4:50 PM — carrying the day's high as it stands. The final report, covering the full climate day, posts around 2:32 AM. Kalshi settles the New Orleans market on The Weather Company's reporting of that final number, in whole degrees.

Two habits worth building: never grade your own day off the preliminary alone, because the final is the one that pays; and remember the climate day runs midnight to midnight standard time, so during daylight saving the official day runs 1 AM to 1 AM on the clock — a warm midnight hour can belong to either day, and the report, not your watch, decides which.

Where is New Orleans's official temperature measured?

At Louis Armstrong New Orleans International Airport, station code KMSY. New Orleans's official daily highs and records come from this station.

What time of day does New Orleans usually hit its high?

On a typical fall day, between about 12:12 PM and 4:15 PM, most often in the 3:00 PM hour. 12.5% of November highs are set before noon.

Why is New Orleans's forecast sometimes off by several degrees?

Because it usually isn't, by plains standards — humid air is predictable air. The misses come from the fronts: a November front that stalls north of the lake or arrives six hours early is worth ten degrees. In the official record, November highs at the airport average about 73°, with a standard deviation of 9.0° — that spread is the honest size of a New Orleans November surprise.

Why doesn't my weather app match New Orleans's official high?

Your app is usually showing a forecast, a model estimate for your location, or a different sensor. Only Louis Armstrong International's reading becomes the official high.

When does the official New Orleans high come out?

A preliminary climate report usually posts around 4:50 PM. The final report, with the official high for the full climate day, posts around 2:32 AM.
