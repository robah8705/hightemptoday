WEEK 4, PART 1: THE TURN

HighTempToday — week in review

Paper Mode's last gated week ended the way none of its earlier weeks did: Bot Scout finished ahead four days in a row, and the intraday records showed the same pattern three days running — the value was there before 2 PM, and settlement kept only part of it. So the simulation isn't ending. It's splitting into arenas — and as of today, real trading is back alongside it.

Key insights
  • Scout closed the run +$33.10 over its final four days, including its best day of the entire experiment.
  • On each of the last three graded days, half or more of Scout's entries were profitable at some point before 2 PM — the entries are finding value; the exits are where it leaks.
  • Paper Mode v2 turns that observation into a test: three arenas, one shared morning scan, different ways of playing it.
  • Live trading resumed today — small tickets, a human approving every one. Tomorrow: the full scoreboard, losers included.

THE FINISH

Start with the scoreboard nobody at this desk expected to write a week ago.

Bot Scout — the morning model that spent most of Paper Mode losing to a shadow portfolio that simply bought the market favorite — finished the run ahead four straight days:

  • Wed Nov 11: +$4.32 — the first slate under the rebuilt rules (raw model probabilities, a 9¢ price floor)
  • Thu Nov 12: +$2.42 — the only day of the run all three portfolios finished positive
  • Fri Nov 13: +$13.32 — 10 of 24 brackets, Scout's best day of the entire experiment
  • Sat Nov 14: +$13.04 — the final gated slate

That's +$33.10 across the last four days, after losing $237.60 across the four days before them. One honest asterisk, with the full numbers tomorrow: the hot finish was Scout's. Bright's midday disagreement layer did not share it — it lost on two of those days, and its habit of leaping to far brackets kept costing it. Part 2 prints the whole record.

Four good days don't erase fifteen mixed ones, and this journal isn't going to pretend otherwise. But a system that ends its test period improving is a different story from one that ends it flat — and the reason it improved is not a mystery. The rules changed mid-run, on evidence, twice. Part 3 on Wednesday is that story.

THE 2 PM PATTERN

The more interesting discovery was hiding in the intraday records — the bid snapshots this desk banks at every check, all day, for every position.

On each of the last three graded days, half or more of Scout's 24 entries were in profit at some point before 2 PM:

  • Nov 11: 12 of 24 above water — sold at their best pre-2 PM marks, they'd have returned +$38.19 instead of +$4.32
  • Nov 12: 16 of 24 above water, 15 of them up at least 10% — best marks +$46.37 instead of +$2.42
  • Nov 13: 14 of 24 above water — best marks +$35.13 instead of +$13.32

Nobody sells the exact top, and this journal won't claim hindsight prices as a strategy. Read the pattern for what it is: the morning entries keep finding real value early in the day — and riding every position to settlement, which Paper Mode required, gives a large share of it back by evening.

Two cities from Nov 13 show both sides of it. Seattle went in at 13¢, touched 96¢ before 2 PM, and then won at settlement anyway — riding beat the peak. Minneapolis went in at 14¢, peaked at 46¢ (+229% on the ticket), and settled a loser — the whole gain was intraday, and it evaporated. Same morning, same rules, opposite lessons. The question is no longer whether the value appears. It's whether any rule — written down in advance, tested on the full record — can keep more of it without strangling the Seattles.

That question now has its own arena.

THE ARENAS

Paper Mode v1 ended Saturday — by decision, not by passing its test (the test's final verdict is tomorrow's post, and it isn't flattering). What replaces it is Paper Mode v2: the Arena — the simulation continuing as pure data collection, split into three competing books on one shared 5 AM scan, each funded at the same $72 of paper money a day:

  • Arena A — the control. The v1 system, unchanged: Scout's single best-edge pick per city, Bright's midday disagreement pass, everything rides to settlement. Whatever the new books claim, they have to beat this.
  • Arena B — the exit books. Identical entries to Scout's, six different ways of leaving: ride, two fixed profit targets, a trailing stop, a fixed afternoon exit, and a split — half out at a target, half riding. This is the 2 PM pattern, turned into a fair fight.
  • Arena C — the Ladder. A different shape entirely: instead of one bracket per city, a small structure — yes on the two likeliest neighboring brackets, no on the tail above them. It wins smaller and more often, if it wins at all.

The verdict rule is written down in advance, the same discipline as the v1 test: after at least ten slates, a book can be proposed for live use only if it beats Arena A after fees — cumulatively, on most days, and still after its single best day is removed. Nobody moves the goalposts afterward, including us.

And the change underneath all of it: live trading resumed today. Small — $3 tickets, every one proposed by the desk and approved by a human before it exists. The simulation no longer gates the real account; it informs it. The real record, when it has settled results worth printing, will be graded and published with the same rules as the paper one.

WHAT'S NEXT

  • Tomorrow (Part 2): the full scoreboard. Fifteen days, three portfolios, the final standings — and the Paper Mode exit test's verdict, all three legs, in plain English.
  • Wednesday (Part 3): what we changed. The rebuild inside the run: a new forecast anchor, ten amendments, one experiment reversed in two days, a price floor built from 341 graded tickets — and the disagreement problem we haven't solved yet.

The market is still winning the long run. But the last four days, and the pattern underneath them, say the gap is not a mystery anymore — it's a list of specific, testable problems. That's what the arenas are for.
