METHODOLOGY

Where the data comes from, how forecasts are built, and how the record is graded.

LAST UPDATED: NOVEMBER 23, 2026

Every number on this site traces back to a public source, and every result we publish is graded against the one document that settles these markets. This page explains where the data comes from, how the forecasts are built, and how the record is scored — so you can check our work instead of taking our word for it.

The short version

Live temperatures come from National Weather Service observations at each market's settlement station. Markets settle on the NWS Climatological Report for that station, as published via The Weather Company under Kalshi's rules. Forecasts are built from public weather models. Simulated trading is always labeled Paper Mode, and simulated results are never presented as real-money results.

What we track

Kalshi lists a daily high-temperature market for 24 US cities. Each market settles on one specific weather station — Central Park for New York, Midway for Chicago, and so on. The full station list, with how each market settles, is on our city pages and in the settlement explainer.

Live highs

The dashboard's live highs come from ASOS station observations distributed by the National Weather Service, refreshed about every five minutes. A "high so far" is an observation, not a result: stations report on their own schedules, readings can arrive late, and a day's high is not final until the official report prints.

What settles a market

Kalshi's daily-high markets settle on the maximum temperature in the NWS Climatological Report (the CLI report) for the settlement station, published via The Weather Company. The settling number is the whole-degree value in the first final report. Forecasts, live observations, and weather apps do not settle anything — the CLI print does. When the NWS corrects a report, the corrected print is what counts, and we restate our record and log it on the Corrections page.

Forecasts and model odds

Projected highs and bracket probabilities are built from an ensemble of public forecast models, including the National Blend of Models, pulled through public data services. A model probability is an estimate with real error in it — our own scoring (below) shows the market's prices are usually sharper than any single model, ours included. Projected brackets are information, not recommendations.

How the record is graded

Every simulated and real position the journal reports is graded after settlement against the official CLI print for that station — never against a live observation or a forecast. Fees are modeled on Kalshi's published fee schedule. Forecast quality is scored with proper scoring rules (Brier score and log loss) against the market's own prices on the same brackets, so the comparison is apples to apples. The full graded record, losses included, is published in the journal.

Limits

  • Observations can be delayed, missing, or revised; a live high can differ from the final print.
  • Model ensembles share common inputs, so model agreement is not independent confirmation.
  • Past graded performance — simulated or real — does not predict future results.
