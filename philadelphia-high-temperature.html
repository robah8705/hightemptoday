KEY FACTS ABOUT PHILADELPHIA'S HIGH

  • One station sets the number: Philadelphia International Airport (KPHL). Not Center City, not the suburbs, not the reading on your phone.
  • Philadelphia has a late tail. On about a quarter of fall days, the high is still unset at 3 PM. Half of highs land by 2:54 PM.
  • It reports about every 5 minutes, so you can watch the afternoon climb in close to real time.
  • The official high comes out the next morning, in the National Weather Service's daily climate report for Philadelphia.

WHY PHILADELPHIA'S OFFICIAL HIGH COMES FROM THE AIRPORT

Philadelphia's official weather record is kept at Philadelphia International Airport, in the southwest part of the city beside the Delaware River. It's the station the National Weather Service uses for Philadelphia's daily climate report, and the one Kalshi's Philadelphia high-temperature market names in its rules.

A reading from Center City, a suburb or your car is measuring somewhere else, sometimes by a couple of degrees. When someone asks how hot Philadelphia got, the official answer is the airport's number in the next morning's climate report.

THE RULEPhiladelphia's official high is Philadelphia International's high, as printed in the next morning's climate report.

PHILADELPHIA'S LATE TAIL: WHY 3 PM ISN'T THE END

Most Eastern stations have done their climbing by mid-afternoon. Philadelphia often hasn't. On a typical fall day the median high comes at 2:54 PM, but about one day in four is still climbing at 3 PM, and the latest highs land closer to 4 PM.

  • Don't call it early. A flat reading at 2:30 PM is not a finished day in Philadelphia. The last hour of the window still produces new highs.
  • Watch the 5-minute stream. Philadelphia reports about every 5 minutes, so a late push shows up as a steady staircase, not a sudden jump.
  • Fronts break the pattern. On roughly 9% of fall days a front arrives and sets the high early, sometimes before noon.

QUESTIONS ABOUT PHILADELPHIA'S HIGH TEMPERATURE

What is the high temperature in Philadelphia today?

The box at the top of this page shows the high so far today at Philadelphia International Airport, Philadelphia's official weather station, updated from the National Weather Service's live observations. What was Philadelphia's high temperature yesterday?

Yesterday's official Philadelphia high is shown in the top box and in the 7-day table above, straight from the NWS climate report for Philadelphia International.

Where is Philadelphia's official temperature measured?

At Philadelphia International Airport, station code KPHL, in the southwest part of the city along the Delaware River. Philadelphia's official daily highs and records come from this station.

What time of day does Philadelphia usually hit its high?

On a typical fall day, between about 12:54 PM and 3:54 PM, most often in the 2 PM hour. About a quarter of fall highs are still unset at 3 PM, so the late afternoon matters more in Philadelphia than in most Eastern cities.

Why doesn't my weather app match Philadelphia's official high?

Your app is usually showing a forecast, a model estimate for your location, or a different sensor. Only Philadelphia International's reading becomes the official high.

When does the official Philadelphia high come out?

A preliminary climate report usually posts in the late afternoon. The final report, with the official high for the full day, comes out the next morning.
