TRENTON: THE FALL LINE'S SMALL STATION

Trenton sits where the Delaware becomes tidal and the Piedmont falls to the coastal plain — the fall line that decided where half the East Coast's old cities went. Its weather is the seam's weather: part Philadelphia, part interior, with the river running through it.

  • Cold-air damming's edge. Shallow cold air banking against the Piedmont reaches Trenton before it reaches the coast. Dammed days sit raw and capped at the airport while stations an hour south are mild — the same wedge that haunts Atlanta, in a New Jersey accent.
  • River valley fog and drainage. The Delaware valley collects cold air and fog on calm fall nights. The airport, just above the valley floor, can start the day in a different air mass from the river towns below it.
  • A small station's paperwork. Trenton is where this desk watched a climate print change its mind: in November 2026 grading, the day's official high in the final report differed from what the station's transmitted record showed, flipping the market's outcome after the fact. Small office, small station, full-sized settlement risk — the desk's Trenton note is the same as its Louisville rule: the print settles.

You can see the shape of it in the tables above: over the last 30 days, Trenton-Mercer Airport's official highs ran from 46° to 74°, averaging 62.7°. That is Trenton's character in one line — the rest of this page is about trading it honestly.

THE WRONG-STATION TRAP: TWO CAPITALS, ONE OFFICE, SEVERAL NUMBERS

  • Philadelphia International (KPHL), 30 miles southwest, is the giant neighbor — same forecast office, similar weather, its own separate market. Its number is not Trenton's.
  • Northeast Philadelphia (KPNE) and Princeton-area stations bracket the airport from both directions along the corridor, each with a slightly different river-and-ridge story.
  • Downtown Trenton sensors sit down in the river valley's warmth, below the airport's fall-line perch.

Trenton's market is a small station with big neighbors, and its climate report comes from an office whose attention is usually on the giant down the river. Read the right report, for the right airport.

WHEN TRENTON'S NUMBER BECOMES OFFICIAL

Trenton's climate report comes from the National Weather Service office in Philadelphia, and it lands in two steps. A preliminary report posts in the late afternoon — today's came at 4:25 PM — carrying the day's high as it stands. The final report, covering the full climate day, posts around 1:05 AM. Kalshi settles the Trenton market on The Weather Company's reporting of that final number, in whole degrees.

Two habits worth building: never grade your own day off the preliminary alone, because the final is the one that pays; and remember the climate day runs midnight to midnight standard time, so during daylight saving the official day runs 1 AM to 1 AM on the clock — a warm midnight hour can belong to either day, and the report, not your watch, decides which.
