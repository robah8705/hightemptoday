WHAT A KALSHI WEATHER TRADE REALLY COSTS

Kalshi's fees are small, but they're not simple. They change with the price, they round up on every order, they're zero on some orders and not others, and you pay again if you sell early. Here's all of it in plain English, with a fee table at real weather prices and a real order history to show how it adds up.

By Buzz, who trades Kalshi's daily-high temperature markets under that name in Kalshi's own chat rooms. Published Thursday, November 19, 2026.

KEY INSIGHTS

  • A market order costs about 1 to 2 cents per contract. The fee follows the price and peaks at 50¢, where it's 1.75¢ per contract.
  • Every order rounds up to the next cent, so tiny orders pay the most per contract. One contract at 10¢ pays a 1¢ fee: 10% of the cost.
  • Resting limit orders pay nothing on weather markets today. Our own Denver order history shows a $0.00 maker fee.
  • Holding to settlement is free; selling early is a second fee. Trading in and out is where fees add up.

01

HOW THE FEE IS CALCULATED

Kalshi's standard trading fee is 7% of price × (1 − price), times the number of contracts, rounded up to the next cent.

fee = round up( 0.07 × contracts × price × (1 − price) )

Price is in dollars, so a 37¢ contract is 0.37. Price × (1 − price) is biggest at 50¢ and shrinks toward the ends, so a coin-flip bracket costs the most to trade and a 5¢ long shot or a 95¢ near-certainty costs the least.

Worked by hand for 20 contracts at 37¢: 0.07 × 20 × 0.37 × 0.63 = $0.326, which rounds up to a $0.33 fee on a $7.40 position.

There's no fee when a market settles, and no membership fee. Kalshi publishes the full rules, including the few series with their own fee multipliers, on its fee schedule.

03

WHY SMALL ORDERS PAY MORE

Because the round-up happens on every order, a tiny order can pay a fee much bigger than the formula suggests. One contract at 10¢ has a raw fee of 0.63¢. It rounds up to a full cent, which is 10% of the 10¢ you paid. A hundred contracts at the same price pay $0.63, or 0.63¢ each.

For beginners trading a few contracts at a time, that rounding is often most of the fee. It's one reason to place one considered order instead of five nervous little ones.

THE RULEOn small orders, the rounding is the fee. Fewer, deliberate orders cost less than many tiny ones.
04

MAKER VS TAKER: THE FEE THAT'S ZERO ON WEATHER

An order that fills right away is a "taker" and pays the fee above. A limit order that rests on the book and fills later is a "maker," and on Kalshi's weather markets today it pays nothing.

Kalshi charges maker fees only on the series it lists on its fee schedule, at a quarter of the taker rate. Its daily temperature markets aren't on that list, and our own order history shows it:

The trade-off: a resting order may never fill, or may fill only after the price has moved against you. A taker order gets you in now, for a fee. Which list a market is on can change, so check the "Fees" column in your own order history.

THE RULEIf you're not in a hurry, rest a limit order. On weather markets today, patience is free.
05

YOU PAY AGAIN WHEN YOU SELL

The fee is charged on every trade, buying or selling. Hold to settlement and you pay once; sell early and you pay twice.

Five orders on one bracket: two buys and three sells, about $1.96 in. The fees across those five orders came to 13 cents, while the trade itself finished down 7 cents. On a small position, trading in and out cost more in fees than the trade lost.

There's a second, quieter cost: the spread. Buying at the ask and selling at the bid means paying the gap between them on top of the fees. On a thin ladder, that gap can be bigger than the fee.

06

HOW FEES MOVE YOUR BREAK-EVEN

A contract's price is the chance it needs to win just to break even, and the fee pushes that bar a little higher. Twenty contracts at 37¢ cost $7.40 plus a $0.33 fee, so $7.73, or 38.65¢ a contract. That bracket now needs to win about 38.7% of the time to break even, not 37%.

HOW TO PAY LESS

  • Rest limit orders when you're not in a hurry. On weather markets they currently pay no fee.
  • Place fewer, larger orders instead of many one-contract clicks, so the round-up doesn't eat you alive.
  • Decide your exit before you enter. Every early sale is another fee and another spread.
  • Mind the middle. Brackets near 50¢ carry the highest fee per contract.
  • Check your order history. The "Fees" column is the truth about what you've paid.

QUESTIONS ABOUT KALSHI FEES

How much are Kalshi fees on weather markets?

A market (taker) order pays roughly 1 to 2 cents per contract: the fee is 0.07 × contracts × price × (1 − price), rounded up to the next cent, and it peaks at 50¢. On weather markets today, a limit order that rests on the book and fills later (a maker order) pays no fee. There is no fee when a market settles.

What is the Kalshi fee formula?

Fee = round up(0.07 × C × P × (1 − P)), where C is the number of contracts and P is the price in dollars. For example, 20 contracts at 37¢: 0.07 × 20 × 0.37 × 0.63 = $0.326, rounded up to $0.33.

Does Kalshi charge maker fees on weather markets?

Not today. Maker fees apply only to the series Kalshi lists on its fee schedule, and its daily temperature markets aren't on that list. A real resting limit order on a Denver daily-high market on Nov 19, 2026 shows a fee of $0.00. The list can change, so check your own order history.

Is there a fee when a Kalshi weather market settles?

No. Kalshi charges no settlement fee. If you hold a position to settlement, the fee you paid to get in is the only trading fee on it.

Why was my Kalshi fee so high on a small order?

Because the fee is rounded up to the next cent on every order. One contract at 10¢ has a raw fee of about 0.6¢, which rounds up to a full cent: 10% of what you paid. The same price at 100 contracts costs about 0.63¢ per contract.
