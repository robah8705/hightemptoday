SEATTLE IN LATE NOVEMBER: WHAT THE RECORD SAYS

Across 472 November days in the official record from 2011 to 2026, Seattle-Tacoma International's highs averaged 52.0°, with a median of 52° — but one November day in ten came in at 45° or colder, and one in ten reached 59° or warmer. That spread, a standard deviation of 5.5°, is the honest shape of a Seattle November.

This November has run warm: through Tue Nov 24, the month's official highs average 55.0°, 3.0° above that 15-year November average. Zoom in on this exact stretch of the calendar — November 20 through 30, over the last seven years — and the official highs ran from 39° to 59°, with a median of 48°.

WHY SEATTLE'S OFFICIAL HIGH COMES FROM THE AIRPORT

Seattle's official weather record is kept at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport, on a plateau south of the city at about 430 feet, between Puget Sound to the west and Lake Washington to the east. It's the station the National Weather Service uses for Seattle's daily climate report, and the one Kalshi's Seattle high-temperature market names in its rules.

Sea-Tac's plateau is a deliberate kind of representative — above the valley fog, inside the marine air, close enough to both bodies of water to feel them. Downtown and valley stations below it run warmer on sunny afternoons and colder on still nights; the plateau splits the difference, officially.

THE RULESeattle's official high is Sea-Tac's high, as printed in the NWS climate report.

SEATTLE: MARINE AIR AND A LATE, GENTLE PEAK

Seattle's weather is written by water: the Sound, the lake, and the Pacific behind the Olympics. Marine air is the default state, and the city's temperature drama is mostly about how long the sun gets to argue with it.

  • The marine push. Onshore flow off Puget Sound moderates everything — it caps summer heat, softens fall cold, and on strong days arrives as a visible front of cooler air that flattens the afternoon where it stands.
  • The plateau effect. Sea-Tac's 430 feet lift it out of the valley's cold pools and fog. Autumn mornings often start warmer at the airport than in the valleys below — an inversion that burns off into a normal day by noon.
  • The convergence zone. When air flowing around the Olympics reunites north or south of the city, it builds a band of cloud and showers that can sit on one neighborhood and miss the airport entirely — Seattle's most local weather feature, and a standing argument for reading the station, not the city.

You can see the shape of it in the tables above: over the last 30 days, Seattle-Tacoma International's official highs ran from 43° to 67°, averaging 54.9°. That is Seattle's character in one line — the rest of this page is about trading it honestly.

THE WRONG-STATION TRAP: THE VALLEY BELOW THE PLATEAU

  • Boeing Field (KBFI), a few miles north in the Duwamish valley, is Seattle's in-city airport — lower, more urban, warmer on sunny afternoons, and not the settlement station.
  • Paine Field (KPAE) in Everett, 25 miles north, sits in the convergence zone's favorite seat and lives a cloudier life than Sea-Tac.
  • Renton (KRNT) at the lake's south end reads the valley floor's version — colder mornings, warmer still afternoons.

Seattle's stations stack by elevation and water, valley to plateau to north county. The market reads the plateau, and the plateau is its own mild country.

WHEN SEATTLE'S NUMBER BECOMES OFFICIAL

Seattle's climate report comes from the National Weather Service office in Seattle, and it lands in two steps. A preliminary report posts in the late afternoon — today's came at 6:21 PM — carrying the day's high as it stands. The final report, covering the full climate day, posts around 1:26 AM. Kalshi settles the Seattle market on The Weather Company's reporting of that final number, in whole degrees.

Two habits worth building: never grade your own day off the preliminary alone, because the final is the one that pays; and remember the climate day runs midnight to midnight standard time, so during daylight saving the official day runs 1 AM to 1 AM on the clock — a warm midnight hour can belong to either day, and the report, not your watch, decides which.

What time of day does Seattle usually hit its high?

On a typical fall day, between about 12:23 PM and 5:01 PM, most often in the 3:00 PM hour. 12.0% of November highs are set before noon.

Why is Seattle's forecast sometimes off by several degrees?

Because it usually isn't, by much — marine climates are steady climates. The misses come from cloud: a convergence-zone band or a marine layer that breaks two hours late is worth the whole day's small range, twice over. In the official record, November highs at the airport average about 52°, with a standard deviation of 5.5° — that spread is the honest size of a Seattle November surprise.

Why doesn't my weather app match Seattle's official high?

Your app is usually showing a forecast, a model estimate for your location, or a different sensor. Only Seattle-Tacoma International's reading becomes the official high.
