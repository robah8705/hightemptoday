The Seattle print deserves its own paragraph, because it's the real thing. At 6:55 PM ET — after Seattle's high was effectively in the books — someone paid 99¢ for 4,211 contracts of the 54–55° bracket: roughly $4,169 at risk to collect roughly $42 at settlement. That's not a forecast. That's settlement arbitrage — picking up the last cent on a decided market — and it's what most genuine size in these markets actually is. It won, the way it almost always does, and the scoreboard below shows the whole species of it.

The day's biggest accumulation tells the opposite story. Across the full session, 41,779 taker-Yes contracts piled into Los Angeles 88° or above — the largest single bracket-and-side pile in any city. It lost: Los Angeles never got there. A pile that big feels like information. On Monday it was just a crowd, agreeing loudly, in the wrong direction.

Win rate is counted once per market — the dominant taker side — never per contract, so a single huge print can't stuff the ballot. P&L is per contract, before fees. Buckets with too few markets are flagged, not hidden: the $500+ row above rests on only 13 markets and proves nothing yet.

WHAT THIS MEANS — AND WHAT IT DOESN'T

  • It doesn't mean "follow the 40¢+ flow." Most of that flow is settlement arbitrage — professionals harvesting the last few cents on markets whose weather already happened. By the time a print exists to follow, the information in it has been spent. There is no live version of this page, on purpose.
  • It does mean the cheap seats are entertainment. Under 40¢, Monday's takers paid for hope at above its own implied price in every band — worst in the 10–39¢ "realistic longshot" zone, where a bracket feels plausible enough to size into and still needs the day to break exactly right.
  • It means size needs a dollar sign. "4,856 contracts" and "$48.56" are the same trade. Any whale-watching that counts contracts without pricing them will show you stubs and call them whales — which is, not coincidentally, how most whale content works.
  • And it means crowds pile up wrong, too. The day's largest accumulation — nearly 42,000 contracts on Los Angeles 88°+ — lost every cent of its stake. Flow is a record of what people did. Settlement is the record of what was true. This page exists to keep the two in that order.

For the mechanics underneath all of it: how these markets settle is the referee every number above answers to, and the fee calculator prices what flow like Monday's paid for the privilege. Our own picks get graded with the same lack of mercy in the journal.

HOW THIS PAGE IS MADE

The tape is Kalshi's own public trade record for the 24 daily-high temperature markets — every trade, pulled in full, then matched market by market against Kalshi's published settlement results. Monday's recap covers 53,299 trades across 134 markets; no market is included until its result is official, and no number on this page is estimated.

Why it publishes in the early afternoon: western markets settle late. Monday's tape could not be fully scored until about 12:30 PM ET on Tuesday, when the last results posted. A recap claiming to be final at 9 AM would be inventing the western third of its own story — so this one waits, and says so. That's the cadence to expect: yesterday's tape, graded and final, early each afternoon.
