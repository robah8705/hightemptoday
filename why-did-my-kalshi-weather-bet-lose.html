YOUR WEATHER BET LOST. HERE'S WHY.

You were sure. The temperature looked right, maybe your position was even up for a while, and then the market settled against you. Almost every one of those losses comes down to one of seven reasons. We know them because our own desk has hit most of them, and we publish every graded trade, losers included.

By Buzz·PUBLISHED Thursday, November 19, 2026·UPDATED November 19, 2026·9 MIN READ

KEY INSIGHTS

  • Only one number pays: the official high for the station named in your market's rules, from the NWS daily climate report, in whole degrees. Your app, the live feed and the price on your screen are all previews.
  • The live feed can be right and you can still lose. The climate report is built from the station's full record and published as a whole number. We've seen it land more than a degree away from the highest live reading.
  • A daily high can only go up. If the station already printed above your bracket before you bought, the bet was dead on arrival.
  • Sometimes nothing went wrong. In our tracking, the market favorite won about 6 times in 10. The other 4 are normal, not a malfunction.

01

YOU WERE WATCHING THE WRONG THERMOMETER

Short answer: each market settles on one named station, and it's often not the one your app shows. Weather apps show a forecast, a model estimate for your location, or the nearest sensor. The market only cares about the station in its rules.

  • Chicago settles on Midway, not O'Hare.
  • New York settles on Central Park, not JFK or LaGuardia, and Central Park reports only once an hour.
  • Los Angeles settles on LAX, on the coast. Downtown has its own station and usually runs warmer; in our own notes, LAX runs about 4 to 6 degrees cooler than downtown forecasts.

Each of our city pages shows which station settles that city and how it behaves.

THE RULEBefore you judge a result, name the station in the market's rules. If your number came from anywhere else, it was never in the running.
02

THE LIVE FEED ISN'T THE OFFICIAL RECORD

Short answer: the climate report's high is built from the station's full record and published as a whole number, so it can differ from the highest live reading you saw. The live feed shows readings in tenths, at the moments the station reports. The official high comes from the station's continuous record, including its one-minute data.

We've watched this decide our own trades, in both directions:

CHICAGO · NOV 5, 2026 · PAPER TRADE

The live feed never showed Midway above 53.6°. Our 55–56° pick looked dead all afternoon. The official climate report printed 55°, inside the bracket, and it won.

DALLAS · NOV 7, 2026 · PAPER TRADE

Our live feed topped out at 69.1°. The climate report printed 69°, inside the 68–69° bracket, and it paid.

Those two went our way. The same mechanism works against you just as easily: a live reading that sat in your bracket all afternoon can publish one degree outside it. That's why our journal grades every trade the next morning, from the official report, never from the feed.

THE RULEThe feed tells you how the day is going. The report tells you how it ended. Only the report pays.
03

THE BRACKET WAS ALREADY DEAD WHEN YOU BOUGHT IT

Short answer: the daily high can only go up during the day, so once the station prints above your bracket, your bracket can't win. It's easy to miss when the overnight hours did it. A warm night or an early-morning reading can already sit above a bracket the market still offers for sale.

OUR SCOUT · NOV 2–6, 2026 · PAPER TRADES

13 of our AI scout's picks were already dead when it ran its scan: an overnight or early-morning reading had already risen past the bracket it went on to choose. Every one of them lost. We kept all 13 on the record, because deleting them would make the method look better than it is.

THE RULEBefore you buy, check the high so far today. If it's already above the top of your bracket, there is nothing left to win.
04

THE DAY TURNED AFTER YOU STOPPED LOOKING

Short answer: a price that's up in the afternoon is a mood, not a result. The market can like your bracket at 2:45 PM and the official high can still land somewhere else. Some stations also peak late; in Philadelphia, about a quarter of fall highs are still unset at 3 PM.

SAN ANTONIO · NOV 6, 2026 · PAPER TRADE

Bought at 2¢. By 2:45 PM the bid had reached 17¢, a paper gain of 750%. Then the official high printed 74°, outside the bracket, and it settled at zero.

Two habits help: know your station's usual peak window (each city page has it), and decide in advance what you'll do if the price runs before the day is done.

THE RULEUntil the station is past its usual peak window, the day is still live, whatever the price says.
05

THE SETTLEMENT DAY RUNS ON STANDARD TIME

Short answer: the official day runs midnight to midnight local standard time, so during daylight saving time it's 1 AM to 1 AM on your clock. A warm reading at 12:30 AM in July belongs to the previous day's settlement, not the new one.

This one mostly bites at night, after warm evenings and around the clock changes in spring and fall. Our settlement explainer walks through it.

THE RULEIn summer, the first hour after midnight belongs to yesterday.
06

YOU READ THE PRELIMINARY REPORT

Short answer: the late-afternoon climate report is a preview, and it never settles anything. The National Weather Service issues a preliminary report for the day so far, usually in the late afternoon. The high can still rise after it posts. Only the first final report, published the next morning, counts.

Corrections published after the market's expiration don't reopen it either. The full chain, including when settlement can be held while reports are reconciled, is in how Kalshi weather markets settle.

THE RULEWait for the final report before you call a winner, including when you're the winner.
07

NOTHING WENT WRONG: FAVORITES LOSE

Short answer: a 60¢ favorite is expected to lose about 4 times in 10. Sometimes the station did exactly what the market thought it might, just not what you hoped.

THE MARKET FAVORITE · OCT 31–NOV 6, 2026 · TRACKED ON PAPER

We tracked buying the market's favorite bracket in every city for a week. It won 102 of 168 cities, about 60.7%. It cost $459.23 and paid back $458.00. The favorite was priced almost exactly right, which means its losses were part of the price all along.

THE RULEJudge a loss by the process, not the outcome. A good bet can lose, and a loss alone doesn't mean anything broke.

HOW TO CHECK WHAT ACTUALLY SETTLED

The morning after the trading day, take five minutes:

  1. Open your market's rules and note the station and the source.
  2. Find that station's official high: The Weather Company publishes it on its Kalshi portal (weather.com/kalshi), taken from the NWS daily climate report.
  3. Compare it with your bracket. Whole degrees only.
  4. If something still looks wrong, compare with the station's own climate report. Our city pages list each station's recent official highs.

Then write down which of the seven reasons it was. After a few weeks, your own list will tell you more than any guide.

QUESTIONS AFTER A LOSS

Why did my Kalshi weather bet lose when my weather app showed the right temperature?

Your app wasn't measuring the settlement station. Kalshi settles on one named station's official high from the NWS daily climate report, in whole degrees. Apps show forecasts, model estimates or other sensors, and they can show your number all day without the official record ever printing it.

My position showed a profit during the day, then I lost. Why?

A profit on the screen is a price, not an outcome. The price can rise and still settle at zero if the official high lands outside your bracket. And the screen's "up" figure is often based on the last trade, not on what a buyer would actually pay you right now; check the bid.

Can I appeal a Kalshi weather settlement?

Settlement follows the first final climate report for the station named in the market's rules. Later corrections don't reopen a settled market, except in a narrow case where Kalshi itself holds expiration because it judges the first value materially wrong. If you think something is off, compare the market's rules, The Weather Company's published value and the station's climate report first.

How do I check what my Kalshi weather market settled on?

The morning after, check the official high for the station named in your market's rules: The Weather Company publishes it on its Kalshi portal (weather.com/kalshi), and it comes from the NWS daily climate report for that station. Our city pages show each station's recent official highs.

Does the preliminary climate report settle the market?

No. The preliminary report posts in the late afternoon and describes the day so far. Only the first final report, published the next morning, settles the market.
