WEEK 2: THE WEEK WE STOPPED TRADING (ON PURPOSE)

HighTempToday trade journal — Week in Review, Oct 26–30, 2026

Week 2 lost money — not catastrophically, and not mysteriously. Systematically. So on Friday we closed every position we could, and on Saturday we stopped trading real money entirely. This is the week the system's own builders benched it, and why that's the most honest trade we've made yet.

THE WEEK IN SHORT

Five more slates ran under Execution Plan v2, with two new pieces live: Stop-Loss v1.1 got its first real week, and Bot Scout v2 — the rebuilt scouting prompt — took its first test runs on Thursday and Friday. The machinery all worked. Orders filled where they should, resting exits fired where they could, stops executed where a market existed to execute into.

The predictions didn't. That's the whole review, really — but a journal that only prints conclusions isn't a journal, so here are the specifics.

WHAT ACTUALLY WENT WRONG

The arithmetic was right. The inputs were unproven. Position sizing, bracket math, exit math — the calculator did exactly what it was told, all week. What failed was upstream of the calculator: the probabilities we fed it. Across the final two days, our scored scout candidates went 1-for-13. Again and again, the day's actual high landed warmer than our working number, and brackets we priced as live chances were dead by early afternoon. A calculator is only as honest as what you type into it. We were typing in estimates dressed up as probabilities.

We were trading too many markets. Slates of five legs, eight legs — more cities than our data or our attention honestly justified. Focus is a risk control too, and we weren't applying it. That changes now: two or three markets a day, chosen well, beats eight chosen hopefully.

Some deaths can't be stopped out of. Friday made the point three times: positions killed by a single station print, in markets where no bid existed to sell into. A stop-loss needs a buyer. Entry timing — not being in the wrong bracket when the decider print lands — is the only real protection there, and it's a lesson about selection, not exits.

It wasn't all lowlights. The exit machinery earned its keep where it could: Monday, Boston's resting +50% exit fired exactly as designed and banked +$1.40 (+51%) before the city's peak window even opened. Wednesday — our worst day, −$9.93 realized on closed legs — Miami's new split exit still carved a small banked win (+$0.64) out of a position that later died. The plumbing works. The forecasting is what has to earn its way back.

THE DECISION: PAPER MODE

Friday afternoon (Oct 30) we closed everything closable. The account sits in cash — $28.60 — with no resting orders and no open positions we chose to keep.

Starting Saturday (Oct 31), the system runs in Paper Mode, indefinitely:

  • Every morning at 10 AM, the scout still scans all 24 cities and names a candidate in each — the full slate, no shortcuts.
  • Every pick is tracked at real market prices on $3 paper tickets, and every position rides to settlement. No cherry-picking exits after the fact.
  • The scout's picks and a second, independent set of reads are graded separately against what the market priced — so we can see whose inputs are off, and by how much.
  • Paper Mode ends when the combined picks consistently beat market pricing on a meaningful sample. Not a good day. Not a good week. A sample.

Week 3 will be all paper, and this journal will report it labeled as paper. If the inputs can't beat the market with fake money, they don't deserve real money. If they can, we'll have the data to prove it — which is the thing we should have built before scaling up in the first place.

THE PLAYBOOK, HONESTLY

We built a 90-page playbook to sell this system — the prompts, the rules, the whole method. This week we made the call we've been circling: the launch is paused.

We thought we had it — the algorithm, the arithmetic, the logic. What the last two weeks actually show is a system that's losing more than it's winning, because its inputs aren't proven yet. A playbook is a promise that a method works. Right now, our own record doesn't back that promise, and we're not selling a promise we can't keep. The book stays built. The launch waits for the proof — and Paper Mode is how the proof gets made, in public, in this journal.

The arithmetic was right. The inputs were unproven. You don't sell the map while you're still checking whether it matches the territory.
