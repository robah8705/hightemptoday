KEY FACTS ABOUT WASHINGTON'S HIGH

  • One station sets the number: Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (KDCA). Not the suburbs, not the reading on your phone.

WHY WASHINGTON'S OFFICIAL HIGH COMES FROM THE AIRPORT

Washington's official weather record is kept at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport, on the Potomac River in Arlington, directly across from the capital's downtown. It's the station the National Weather Service uses for Washington's daily climate report — the city's official station since the 1940s — and the one Kalshi's Washington high-temperature market names in its rules.

National's site is a warm pocket by construction: low elevation, riverfront, pavement on every side, and the urban heat of two downtowns — Washington's and Arlington's — feeding it. The region's other stations sit higher, greener or farther inland, and read accordingly.

THE RULEWashington's official high is Reagan National's high, as printed in the NWS climate report.

WASHINGTON: THE RIVERFRONT HEAT POCKET

The capital sits in the Potomac's shallow valley where the fall line meets the tide. Its airport occupies the valley floor's warmest acreage — and the city's weather reputation, from its heavy summers to its mild fall afternoons, is substantially the story of that one riverside site.

  • The urban-river heat pocket. Low elevation, dense surroundings and the river's stored warmth combine to keep National ahead of the region. On calm fall days the airport's high can beat the suburbs' by three to five degrees — a full bracket, courtesy of geography.
  • Bay and river breezes. The Potomac and, behind it, the Chesapeake send afternoon breezes that can trim the peak — but they arrive at National weakened by the city, and the pocket usually wins.
  • Fall-line fronts. Cold fronts cross the region from the Blue Ridge and reach the river fast. A midday front ends National's climb like anywhere — but the pocket's warmth means the morning's fallback high is higher here than inland.

You can see the shape of it in the tables above: over the last 30 days, Reagan National Airport's official highs ran from 52° to 77°, averaging 65.9°. That is Washington's character in one line — the rest of this page is about trading it honestly.

THE WRONG-STATION TRAP: THE SUBURBAN GIANTS

  • Washington Dulles (KIAD), 25 miles west in the Virginia suburbs, is the international gateway and a fine station — higher, greener, more continental, and regularly two to four degrees away from National in either direction. It settles nothing here.
  • BWI (KBWI) belongs to Baltimore's market story, not Washington's — a different city, a different report.
  • College Park (KCGS) and the Maryland suburban stations read the region's greener, higher version of the day.

Washington's metro has three major airports and one riverside number. The market chose the warm pocket across from downtown, and trading it as if it were the suburbs is a standing error.

WHEN WASHINGTON'S NUMBER BECOMES OFFICIAL

Washington's climate report comes from the National Weather Service office in Baltimore/Washington, and it lands in two steps. A preliminary report posts in the late afternoon — today's came at 4:24 PM — carrying the day's high as it stands. The final report, covering the full climate day, posts around 2:30 AM. Kalshi settles the Washington market on The Weather Company's reporting of that final number, in whole degrees.

Two habits worth building: never grade your own day off the preliminary alone, because the final is the one that pays; and remember the climate day runs midnight to midnight standard time, so during daylight saving the official day runs 1 AM to 1 AM on the clock — a warm midnight hour can belong to either day, and the report, not your watch, decides which.

Where is Washington's official temperature measured?

At Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport, station code KDCA. Washington's official daily highs and records come from this station.

What time of day does Washington usually hit its high?

On a typical fall day, between about 12:50 PM and 4:14 PM, most often in the 3:00 PM hour. 8.2% of November highs are set before noon.

Why is Washington's forecast sometimes off by several degrees?

Because the region is the miss — a forecast built for the metro's average misses a station that is deliberately not average. National's pocket runs hot, and the models that smooth it toward the suburbs give away the difference. In the official record, November highs at the airport average about 59°, with a standard deviation of 9.4° — that spread is the honest size of a Washington November surprise.

Why doesn't my weather app match Washington's official high?

Your app is usually showing a forecast, a model estimate for your location, or a different sensor. Only Reagan National Airport's reading becomes the official high.

When does the official Washington high come out?

A preliminary climate report usually posts around 4:24 PM. The final report, with the official high for the full climate day, posts around 2:30 AM.
