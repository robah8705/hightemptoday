MINNEAPOLIS IN LATE NOVEMBER: WHAT THE RECORD SAYS

Across 472 November days in the official record from 2011 to 2026, Minneapolis–St. Paul International's highs averaged 44.0°, with a median of 44° — but one November day in ten came in at 28° or colder, and one in ten reached 60° or warmer. That spread, a standard deviation of 12.6°, is the honest shape of a Minneapolis November.

This November has run warm: through Tue Nov 24, the month's official highs average 53.5°, 9.5° above that 15-year November average. Zoom in on this exact stretch of the calendar — November 20 through 30, over the last seven years — and the official highs ran from 17° to 56°, with a median of 39°.

THE RULEIn Minneapolis, price the range, not the point. A November forecast is a center of a very wide distribution, and the brackets near the middle are where that distribution is flattest.

WHY MINNEAPOLIS' OFFICIAL HIGH COMES FROM THE AIRPORT

The Twin Cities' official weather record is kept at Minneapolis–Saint Paul International Airport, between the two downtowns, at about 840 feet above the Mississippi River valley. It's the station the National Weather Service uses for the Twin Cities' daily climate report, and the one Kalshi's Minneapolis high-temperature market names in its rules.

The airport's bluff-top position, south of both downtowns, gives it a cleaner read of the region's air than the valley stations below — and in Minnesota, the air is the story. No terrain stands between this station and the Arctic, and none stands between it and the Gulf. It simply reports whichever one showed up.

THE RULEThe Twin Cities' official high is MSP International's high, as printed in the NWS climate report.

MINNEAPOLIS: THE CONTINENT'S CROSSROADS

Minneapolis sits near the geographic center of North America, farther from any ocean than any other city on this board. Continental climates don't do moderation: they do whatever the current air mass says, at full strength, until the next one arrives.

  • Arctic outbreaks. November is when the polar tap opens. Highs that fall all day from a midnight maximum, single-digit wind chills by afternoon — the month's cold tail is the real thing, not a rounding error.
  • The last warm surges. The same month can still pull Gulf air north ahead of a storm and print a 65 or 70. Minneapolis's November distribution has two humps, and the forecast question is which one the week belongs to.
  • Snow cover's feedback. Once snow is on the ground, everything changes: sunshine reflects away, highs cap hard, and a forecast made for bare ground misses by ten degrees. The first lasting snow of the season is a regime change, not a weather event.

You can see the shape of it in the tables above: over the last 30 days, Minneapolis–St. Paul International's official highs ran from 30° to 72°, averaging 54.5°. That is Minneapolis's character in one line — the rest of this page is about trading it honestly.

THE WRONG-STATION TRAP: TWO CITIES, ONE AIRPORT, MANY VALLEYS

  • St. Paul Downtown (KSTP) sits down in the Mississippi valley, a few miles east — colder on calm nights, sometimes warmer on still afternoons, never the official number.
  • Crystal (KMIC) and Flying Cloud (KFCM) ring the west and southwest suburbs with their own feeds, each a few degrees of local terrain away from the airport.
  • Downtown Minneapolis sensors ride the heat island of the towers — a couple of degrees of city warmth that the airport never sees.

The metro is a patchwork of bluff, valley and heat island. The market reads one bluff-top station between the downtowns, and its number is the only one that prints.

WHEN MINNEAPOLIS' NUMBER BECOMES OFFICIAL

Minneapolis's climate report comes from the National Weather Service office in the Twin Cities, and it lands in two steps. A preliminary report posts in the late afternoon — today's came at 6:36 PM — carrying the day's high as it stands. The final report, covering the full climate day, posts around 1:11 AM. Kalshi settles the Minneapolis market on The Weather Company's reporting of that final number, in whole degrees.

Two habits worth building: never grade your own day off the preliminary alone, because the final is the one that pays; and remember the climate day runs midnight to midnight standard time, so during daylight saving the official day runs 1 AM to 1 AM on the clock — a warm midnight hour can belong to either day, and the report, not your watch, decides which.

Where is Minneapolis's official temperature measured?

What time of day does Minneapolis usually hit its high?

On a typical fall day, between about 10:56 AM and 4:53 PM, most often in the 3:00 PM hour. 16.4% of November highs are set before noon.

Why is Minneapolis's forecast sometimes off by several degrees?

Because November straddles two climates here, and picking the wrong one — or the wrong day for the change, or forgetting the snow cover — produces the biggest forecast misses on the board. In the official record, November highs at the airport average about 44°, with a standard deviation of 12.6° — that spread is the honest size of a Minneapolis November surprise.

Why doesn't my weather app match Minneapolis's official high?

Your app is usually showing a forecast, a model estimate for your location, or a different sensor. Only Minneapolis–St. Paul International's reading becomes the official high.
