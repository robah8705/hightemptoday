KEY FACTS ABOUT AUSTIN'S HIGH

  • One station sets the number: Austin-Bergstrom International Airport (KAUS). Not the suburbs, not the reading on your phone.
  • Austin is the two-station city in a second sense: its weather record was kept for decades at Camp Mabry, in the heart of town, while the market settles at Bergstrom, the former air base southeast of the city. The two disagree often enough to matter.
  • It reports about every 5 minutes, so you can watch the afternoon climb in close to real time.
  • The print has two steps: a preliminary climate report around 5:35 PM, then the final around 1:25 AM. The market settles on the final.

AUSTIN IN LATE NOVEMBER: WHAT THE RECORD SAYS

Across 472 November days in the official record from 2011 to 2026, Austin-Bergstrom International's highs averaged 72.8°, with a median of 75° — but one November day in ten came in at 58° or colder, and one in ten reached 85° or warmer. That spread, a standard deviation of 10.5°, is the honest shape of a Austin November.

This November has run cool: through Tue Nov 24, the month's official highs average 71.9°, 0.8° below that 15-year November average. Zoom in on this exact stretch of the calendar — November 20 through 30, over the last seven years — and the official highs ran from 45° to 87°, with a median of 70°.

THE RULEIn Austin, price the range, not the point. A November forecast is a center of a very wide distribution, and the brackets near the middle are where that distribution is flattest.

WHY AUSTIN'S OFFICIAL HIGH COMES FROM THE AIRPORT

Austin's market record is kept at Austin-Bergstrom International Airport, southeast of downtown at about 540 feet, on the old Bergstrom Air Force Base grounds where the coastal plain starts to rise toward the Hill Country. It's the station Kalshi's Austin high-temperature market names in its rules, and its climate report is the one that settles the market.

Bergstrom's open, semi-rural setting reads differently from the city it serves — cooler nights, cleaner afternoon peaks, none of downtown's stored heat. Anyone quoting 'Austin's temperature' from a central-city sensor is describing a warmer place than the settlement station.

THE RULEAustin's market high is Austin-Bergstrom's high, as printed in the NWS climate report.

AUSTIN: THE ESCARPMENT'S EASTERN DOOR

Austin straddles the Balcones Escarpment where San Antonio does, a couple of hours northeast — Hill Country to the west, blackland prairie to the east, and the city spread across the seam. Its weather is the negotiation between those two terrains and the Gulf air that visits both.

  • Escarpment downslope. West winds descend from the Hill Country and warm into the city, Bergstrom included. Upslope easterlies do the opposite — low cloud, drizzle and a hard cap. The wind's direction is worth five degrees here.
  • Gulf moisture's reach. Austin is the inland end of the Gulf's daily commute. Humid southerly days build steadily and peak early-to-mid afternoon; the truly hot November surprises come when dry Hill Country air mixes down instead.
  • Two terrains, one metro. The west side's hills and the east side's flat prairie run measurably different afternoons. The airport sits at the boundary, which is precisely why its number surprises people reading either side's sensors.

You can see the shape of it in the tables above: over the last 30 days, Austin-Bergstrom International's official highs ran from 53° to 86°, averaging 73.2°. That is Austin's character in one line — the rest of this page is about trading it honestly.

THE WRONG-STATION TRAP: CAMP MABRY'S LONG SHADOW

  • Camp Mabry (KATT) kept Austin's official record for most of the twentieth century and still reports from in-town, by the river. It is a genuine climate station with real history — and it is not the settlement station for this market. Its numbers run a degree or two from Bergstrom's on many days.
  • Austin Executive (KEDC) northeast and San Marcos (KHYI) south report the metro's edges — prairie readings that drift from the airport's.
  • Downtown and lakeside sensors ride the heat of the city and the cool of the water at once. Colorful, never official.

Austin is the rare market where the 'wrong' station is a beloved institution with a century of records. The market moved to the airport anyway, and the airport's number is the only one that prints.

THE RULEKAUS or it didn't happen. Camp Mabry is history — only Bergstrom is settlement.

WHEN AUSTIN'S NUMBER BECOMES OFFICIAL

Austin's climate report comes from the National Weather Service office in Austin/San Antonio, and it lands in two steps. A preliminary report posts in the late afternoon — today's came at 5:35 PM — carrying the day's high as it stands. The final report, covering the full climate day, posts around 1:25 AM. Kalshi settles the Austin market on The Weather Company's reporting of that final number, in whole degrees.

Two habits worth building: never grade your own day off the preliminary alone, because the final is the one that pays; and remember the climate day runs midnight to midnight standard time, so during daylight saving the official day runs 1 AM to 1 AM on the clock — a warm midnight hour can belong to either day, and the report, not your watch, decides which.

Where is Austin's official temperature measured?

At Austin-Bergstrom International Airport, station code KAUS. Austin's official daily highs and records come from this station.

What time of day does Austin usually hit its high?

On a typical fall day, between about 1:57 PM and 4:30 PM, most often in the 3:00 PM hour. 8.6% of November highs are set before noon.

Why is Austin's forecast sometimes off by several degrees?

Because the escarpment again — wind direction is worth five degrees, fronts stall on the hills, and a forecast centered on the wrong regime misses the high by the width of two brackets. In the official record, November highs at the airport average about 73°, with a standard deviation of 10.5° — that spread is the honest size of a Austin November surprise.

Why doesn't my weather app match Austin's official high?

Your app is usually showing a forecast, a model estimate for your location, or a different sensor. Only Austin-Bergstrom International's reading becomes the official high.

When does the official Austin high come out?

A preliminary climate report usually posts around 5:35 PM. The final report, with the official high for the full climate day, posts around 1:25 AM.
